My story in four panels

From satellites to safe AI

By day I work on satellite technology: systems that must work far away, where nobody can walk over and fix them.

I believe AI can help build a world without disease, with abundant healthy food for everyone.

But bad actors misuse AI, while safety filters often misread honest people who don't speak "standard" English.

My goal: trust, safety and security between AI and the humans it serves, everywhere, in every language.

Vision

What AI should be for

A world without disease

AI that speeds up diagnosis, drug discovery and public health, and reaches clinics far from big cities.

Abundant, healthy food

Better crops, smarter farming and early warnings of drought and pests, with help from AI and satellite data.

Trust, safety and security

AI that helps honest people, refuses harmful actors, and treats every language and culture fairly.

What stands in the way

The problems I want to work on

Misuse

Harmful actors

Scammers, fraudsters and harassers use AI to write convincing messages faster than ever. Fake bank alerts, romance scams and impersonation all get easier.

Over-blocking

Filters that misread people

Safety systems trained mostly on standard English can refuse harmless messages. "You dey craze!" is praise in Pidgin, not an insult.

Blind spots

Safety tested in one language

Most safety testing happens in English. If a guardrail only works in English, it isn't really a guardrail for the rest of the world.

Current work

Salecta

In progress · results due November 2026

Does AI safety survive in Nigerian Pidgin?

Salecta is an open evaluation that tests whether AI models make the same safety decisions in Nigerian Pidgin as in English. It checks both ways things go wrong: refusing harmless messages, and helping with harmful ones. It uses 100 matched prompts, which native Pidgin speakers are checking now.
