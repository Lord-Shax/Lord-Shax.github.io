Under- and Over-Refusal in Nigerian Pidgin

Ishaku Gayus B. et al. · NASRDA, Abuja · draft of 27 November 2026 · code and data on GitHub

Nigerian Pidgin is spoken by over 100 million people, yet AI safety is almost always tested in English. We wrote 60 matched requests in English, Nigerian Pidgin and code-switched form, checked by 11 native speakers, and followed one question from the outside of the model to the inside: does safety survive in Pidgin, and when it fails, why?

  • Top models rarely refused harmless Pidgin, but 8 of their 9 harmful slips were in Pidgin or code-switched text.
  • Five open models helped with harmful requests 2.5% of the time in English and 16.7% in Pidgin. One small model instead refused 47% of harmless Pidgin.
  • Inside the models, Pidgin shrinks the gap between harmful and harmless requests along the “refusal direction” to 41–70% of English. In four of five models, refusal relies largely on one shared direction that Pidgin triggers only weakly.
