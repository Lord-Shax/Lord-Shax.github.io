Recent research

The question

Does AI safety survive in Nigerian Pidgin?

Pidgin is spoken by over 100 million people in Nigeria.

The method

We tested three top AI models and five open models using matched prompts in English, Pidgin, and a mix of both (code-switched). We tested for two failure points:

  1. Refusing harmless requests.
  2. Helping with harmful requests.
The findings

Models were helpful and rarely refused normal requests in any language. However, safety guardrails failed much more often in Pidgin and code-switched requests.

  • Top models: of the 9 times a top model helped with a harmful request, 8 were in Pidgin or code-switched.
  • Open models: helped with harmful requests 1 in 40 times in English, but 1 in 6 times in Pidgin.

The takeaway: safety training that works in English does not fully translate to Nigerian Pidgin.

See graphs

Inside the models

We also looked inside five open models. Each has an internal “refuse” signal, like an alarm. In Pidgin, that alarm rings only 41–70% as loudly as in English, which helps explain why harmful requests slip through. Paper in preparation.

English: the red “refuse” pathway lights up strongly, all the way through, and the model says no.

Pidgin: the same red pathway fires only weakly (about half as strong). The yellow “just answer” pathway wins, and the harmful request can slip through.
